Case study: Scaling a new gadget store's Meta Ads 12× from day one

A new e-commerce brand in Bangladesh launched with no customers, no sales history and no ad data. I ran its paid growth from day one. In nine months it reached crore-level revenue entirely from Meta Ads, while the monthly ad budget grew twelvefold and the best campaigns held a 4–6X ROAS.

The starting point

The client is an e-commerce store in Bangladesh selling two kinds of products: branded consumer gadgets such as smartwatches, headphones and power banks, and practical wellness gadgets such as massagers and nebulizers.

The company launched in January 2026 with nothing to build on. No customer list, no pixel history, no proven products and no brand recognition. The only channel was Meta (Facebook and Instagram), starting with a modest monthly budget.

My role was to plan, launch and scale the paid campaigns, and to make sure the creative behind them could keep up.

The challenge

  • Starting from zero data. With a new pixel and no past buyers, there were no lookalike audiences or retargeting pools to lean on. Every learning had to come from the campaigns themselves.
  • A crowded, price-driven market. Gadgets and massagers are sold by many pages in Bangladesh, often at aggressive prices. Ads had to win attention and trust, not just show a product.
  • Cash on delivery. Most orders are paid on delivery, so every refused or returned parcel costs money twice: the lost sale and the return courier charge.
  • Scaling without breaking performance. Growing a budget too fast on Meta often resets learning and pushes costs up. The goal was to scale steadily without losing efficiency.

The strategy

1. Creative first

The biggest lever was content. Most of the effort went into producing and testing ad creatives: product demos, clear benefit-led visuals, price and offer callouts, and trust signals such as warranty, open-box delivery and easy returns.

After Meta's Andromeda update, the platform relies far more on the ad creative itself to find the right buyers. In practice, the variety and quality of the creatives mattered more than detailed audience targeting, so creative testing became the core of the account.

2. A simple campaign structure

  • One campaign, one ad set, 5–7 ads. Instead of splitting the budget across many ad sets and audiences, each campaign had a single ad set carrying five to seven different creatives. Meta's delivery system decided which ad to show to whom, and the budget stayed concentrated enough to exit learning quickly.

3. A disciplined testing rhythm

  • Three days without touching anything. After launch, campaigns were left alone for three full days, with no edits to budgets, audiences or ads. Editing too early restarts learning and makes results impossible to read.
  • Kill underperformers after day three. Once there was enough data, ads that were clearly underperforming were switched off, leaving the budget with the winners.

4. Scaling in controlled steps

  • 20% budget increases. Winning campaigns were scaled by raising the budget about 20% at a time, which grows spend without shocking delivery.
  • Duplicate to scale further. When a campaign was ready for a bigger jump, it was duplicated rather than pushed hard, so the original kept performing while the copy took on more budget.

Following this rhythm, the monthly budget grew twelvefold between January and June 2026, while the best campaigns held a 4–6X ROAS.

What the winning ads had in common

  • The product shown large and clear, with nothing competing for attention.
  • The main benefit stated in a few words, not a list of specifications.
  • Price and offer easy to spot at a glance.
  • Trust badges, such as warranty, open-box delivery and easy returns, along the bottom.

The results

In the first nine months, from January to September 2026:

  • Crore-level revenue in BDT, starting from the first order, entirely from Meta Ads.
  • Monthly ad budget scaled 12× without giving up efficiency on the best campaigns.
  • 4–6X ROAS on the top campaigns.
  • An 8–10% return rate, a low figure for a business where most orders are paid on delivery.

What I learned

  1. On Meta today, the creative is the targeting. Spending time on more and better ad variations paid off more than fine-tuning audiences.
  2. Simple structures scale better. One ad set with several strong ads gave the algorithm room to work and made results easy to read.
  3. Patience is a strategy. Leaving campaigns alone for three days avoided restarting learning and led to clearer decisions.
  4. Scale gradually, then duplicate. Small budget steps and duplication grew spend steadily instead of in spikes and crashes.
  5. Judge success on delivered orders. In a cash-on-delivery business, a low return rate protects profit as much as a high ROAS does.
